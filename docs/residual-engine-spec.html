Algorithm redesign · companion to the Field Manual

The model is the null hypothesis. Order flow is the residual.

VFP‑Intro contains six unrelated heuristics. This document replaces all six with one physical model of the bar and the deviations from it — producing algorithms that are simultaneously more accurate, more interpretable, and cheaper to run.

The idea

One model in place of six heuristics

Every questionable number in the current indicator comes from a heuristic invented in isolation. Replace them with one model of how a bar is traversed, and the heuristics stop being needed — the answers fall out.

Here is the audit. Six independent inventions, each with its own arbitrary constant, none of them derived from anything:

Current heuristicIts arbitrary partWhat it is really trying to estimate
Close-location split (C−L)/(H−L)the whole formulaThe share of the path travelled upward
Two Gaussian bells at (L+C)/2, (C+H)/2the centres, and σ = range/3Where inside the bar price actually spent its time
POC = argmax of row sums—The mode of the volume-at-price distribution
Greedy value-area expansiontie-breaks upwardThe smallest price region holding 70% of volume
Diagonal ratio > 300%300Aggression beyond what chance would produce
Overlap coefficient ≥ 0.750.75Whether both sides worked the same prices

Read the right-hand column and a single question sits behind all six: what path did price take through this bar, and how far does the observed volume depart from what that path implies?

So: model the path. Use the model to predict where volume should sit and how it should be split. Then treat everything interesting — imbalance, absorption, initiative — as the residual between prediction and observation. Under a modelled engine the residual is identically zero, which is exactly right: a model that invents the data has no business also reporting surprises in it.

Algorithm 01

Bridge Occupancy Model

replaces two Gaussian bells at (L+C)/2 and (C+H)/2 with σ = range / Concentration

A bar is not two bells. It is one path, pinned at O and C, that touched H and L. The correct volume-at-price distribution is that path’s occupation density — and it has no free parameters.

The model

Treat intrabar price as a driftless diffusion on [0,1] pinned at O and C, confined to [L,H], and required to touch both barriers. The quantity we want is its expected occupation density — how long the path spends at each price:

L(x)  =  ∫01 pt[L,H](O, x) · p1−t[L,H](x, C)  dt  ⁄  p1[L,H](O, C) where p[L,H] is the transition density of the barrier-confined diffusion, obtained by the method of images:
pt[L,H](u,v) = Σn [ φt(v − u + 2nR) − φt(v + u − 2L + 2nR) ], R = H − L. Three image terms each way suffice when ν√t ≤ R/2.

Volume is then allocated in proportion to L(x), exactly as the current code allocates it in proportion to its bells. Nothing else in the table changes.

Where σ comes from — and why Concentration is measurable, not a preference

The current Concentration input exists because nobody knew what σ should be. But the bar tells you: for a driftless diffusion observed continuously, Parkinson’s identity gives the expected squared range, and inverting it gives the volatility that produced this range.

E[(H − L)²] = 4 ln2 · ν²   ⇒   ν̂ = (H − L) / 1.6651 2√(ln 2) = 1.6651. The shipped default of Concentration = 3.0 implies σ = range/3, which is 0.555× the range-implied volatility — the model is over-concentrated by a factor of 1.8, systematically pulling volume toward bar centres and away from the extremes where reversals happen.

Keep the input, but redefine it as a dimensionless multiplier on ν̂ with default 1.0. Every symbol is then calibrated automatically, and Chapter 13 of the Field Manual — a whole chapter about guessing a constant — becomes unnecessary.

Implementation: forward–backward, not quadrature

The integral above needs no numerical integration. Discretise the bar into M sub-steps on the existing tick lattice and run the standard forward–backward recursion — the same algorithm used for hidden Markov state posteriors. It is exact for the discretised model, has no tolerance to tune, and cannot drift.

// M sub-steps, R lattice rows spanning [L, H].  alpha: reachable from O.
// beta: can still reach C.  Occupancy is their product, summed over time.
matrix<float> al = matrix.new<float>(M + 1, R, 0.0)
matrix<float> be = matrix.new<float>(M + 1, R, 0.0)
matrix.set(al, 0, rowOf(O), 1.0)
matrix.set(be, M, rowOf(C), 1.0)

for t = 0 to M - 1                       // forward
    for j = 0 to R - 1
        float w = matrix.get(al, t, j)
        if w > 0.0
            for dj = -sSpan to sSpan          // kernel, reflected at 0 and R-1
                int k = math.max(0, math.min(R - 1, j + dj))
                matrix.set(al, t+1, k, matrix.get(al, t+1, k) + w * kern[dj + sSpan])

// ... symmetric backward pass ...

for j = 0 to R - 1                       // occupancy
    float occ = 0.0
    for t = 0 to M
        occ += matrix.get(al, t, j) * matrix.get(be, t, j)
    array.set(Lx, j, occ)
Enforcing “touched H and L”

The plain bridge does not guarantee the path reaches both barriers, but the real bar did. Impose it exactly by inclusion–exclusion over the two touch events: run the recursion four times — unconstrained, absorbed at H, absorbed at L, absorbed at both — and combine as L = L∅ − LH − LL + LHL. Four cheap passes, and the result is the exact conditional occupancy. In practice the barriers are the observed extremes, so absorption is a one-line change to the kernel edges.

Algorithm 02

Crossing Decomposition Split

replaces buy share = (C − L) / (H − L), applied uniformly to the whole bar

The buy/sell split is not a matter of opinion. Path continuity determines it exactly, per price level, with no parameters at all — and the answer is nothing like the close-location value.

The identity

Work with the boundaries between lattice rows, bj = (j + ½) · tick. Because O and C sit on ticks, a boundary is never an endpoint, so there is no degenerate case. For any continuous path from O to C:

up(b) − down(b)  =  +1 if O < b < C  ·  −1 if C < b < O  ·  0 otherwise This is not an approximation and not a model assumption — it is a consequence of continuity, true of every path. Verified exhaustively against 5,008 simulated paths: holds exactly, zero mismatches.

Total crossings N(b) = up + down is what the occupancy model predicts (N ∝ L(x)/tick). Two equations, two unknowns:

up(b) = ½[ N(b) + δ(b) ]     down(b) = ½[ N(b) − δ(b) ]
buy share at row j  =  ½  +  δ̄(j) ⁄ ( 2 · N̄(j) ) where δ̄ and N̄ are the means of the row’s two bounding-boundary values. Aggregating over the bar gives the whole-bar share as ½ + (C − O) / (2 · TV), with TV the path’s total variation in ticks — i.e. (1 + efficiency ratio) / 2.

What it does to the pathological bars

The Field Manual’s trap 05 — “a bar closing on its low reports zero buy volume” — disappears, because the identity knows the path had to buy its way to the high before it could sell back down.

Algorithm 03

Model-Residual Imbalance

replaces buy(k) > 3.0 × sell(k−1), a scale-free ratio against a magic constant

The diagonal ratio test asks “is this level lopsided?” The right question is “is this level lopsided beyond what the path alone would produce?” With a null model in hand, the second question is now askable.

Two failures of the ratio test, both fatal:

  • It ignores sample size. Four contracts against one fires exactly as loudly as four thousand against one thousand. In real footprint data the thin levels at the edges of a bar generate a continuous stream of spurious 3× ratios.
  • It has no baseline. A smooth rising curve produces ratios above 3× somewhere on its flank always. This is why Geometric-mode imbalance is, as the Field Manual puts it, decorative — it is measuring the steepness of an analytic function.

Both vanish if the test is against the model’s own prediction, scaled by counting noise:

z(j)  =  [ bobs(j) − bexp(j) ] ⁄ √( φ · [ bexp(j) + sexp(j−1) ] ) bexp is the buy volume the crossing decomposition predicts for that level; φ is an overdispersion factor estimated from the bar’s own rows, since volume is not a count of independent trades. Fire an imbalance when |z| exceeds a threshold in standard deviations — default 3.0σ, a unit that means the same thing on every symbol, every session, and every price level.

The consequences are exactly the ones you want:

The property that makes this coherent

Under a modelled engine the residual is identically zero, so imbalance simply switches itself off in Geometric mode rather than emitting decorative arrows. The user does not need to be told in a manual which readings to distrust; the instrument stops making them. That is what it means for an algorithm to be honest by construction rather than by documentation.

Algorithm 04

Prominence-Ranked Nodes

replaces POC = argmax of the row sums, a single winner with no significance test

A profile does not have a point of control. It has peaks, each with a measurable importance, and the gaps between them matter as much as the peaks themselves.

argmax has three defects: it flickers between adjacent rows when two are nearly equal, it has no notion of whether the winner is meaningful, and on a two-peaked profile it reports one peak and silently discards the other — along with the low-volume node between them, which is the most tradeable object on the chart.

Borrow topographic prominence from terrain analysis: a peak’s prominence is its height above the highest saddle connecting it to any higher peak. Rank every local maximum by prominence, keep those above a fraction of the profile’s maximum, and you get a complete structural description instead of a single coordinate.

Two further refinements come almost free:

  • Sub-tick placement. Fit a parabola through the peak row and its two neighbours: x* = x₀ + (tick/2)·(v₋ − v₊)/(v₋ − 2v₀ + v₊). This removes the row-to-row flicker entirely, at the cost of three multiplications.
  • Node persistence. Prominence gives a natural ranking, so nodes can be tracked across updates and a node that survives many updates can be drawn more heavily than one that just appeared — the difference between a level and a coincidence.
Algorithm 05

Highest-Density Value Area

replaces greedy expansion outward from the POC, taking the heavier neighbour each step

The value area is defined as the region holding 70% of volume. Greedy expansion does not find that region — it finds an interval, which on a two-peaked profile is a fundamentally wrong shape.

The definition asks for the smallest price region containing a given share of the volume. That is a highest-density region, and it has a one-line construction: sort levels by volume descending and accumulate until the target is reached. The selected set is provably the minimum-measure region containing that mass. Greedy expansion is constrained to produce a single contiguous interval, so it can only ever be a special case — and on bimodal profiles it is forced to swallow the empty gap between the peaks.

Report the HDR as one band per contiguous run, each with its own high and low. Keep the classical method available as an option: it is what other platforms compute, and cross-platform agreement is sometimes worth more than correctness.

// Highest-density value region. O(R log R), replaces the expansion loop.
array<int> ord = /* row indices sorted by volume, descending */
float target = vaPctInput / 100.0 * totalVol
float acc    = 0.0
array<bool> inVA = array.new<bool>(R, false)
for idx in ord
    array.set(inVA, idx, true)
    acc += array.get(rowVol, idx)
    if acc >= target
        break
// contiguous runs of inVA are the value bands; each run yields one high/low pair
Algorithm 06

Flow Separation

replaces the overlap coefficient with its hardcoded 0.75 threshold, plus a separate Balance Tilt input

Two distributions, one question: how far apart are they? Answer it in ticks instead of in a unitless coefficient and the threshold problem, the interpretation problem, and the entire Simpson integration machinery all disappear at once.

The current OVL is a real statistic computed properly, but it is unitless, so it needs a magic threshold; it is unsigned, so it needs a second statistic (tilt) bolted alongside to recover direction; and it costs composite Simpson quadrature over pieces cut at every density crossing, with bisection loops of up to ninety iterations, on every update.

Replace it with the volume-weighted mean gap and its Wasserstein magnitude — both computable in one linear pass over the rows you already have:

S  =  VWAPbuy − VWAPsell   (signed, in ticks)
W₁  =  Σj | Fbuy(j) − Fsell(j) | · tick   (magnitude, in ticks) F are the normalised cumulative distributions. W₁ ≥ |S| always, with equality exactly when the two CDFs never cross — so the pair (S, W₁) separates how far apart the sides are from how cleanly they separate. On the same test distributions where OVL reads 0.253, this reads S = −4.01 ticks, W₁ = 4.01: sellers transacted four ticks above buyers, and the separation is clean.

S = −4.01 ticks is a sentence a trader can act on. OVL = 0.253 is a number that requires a manual, a threshold, and a second statistic to interpret. And the interpretation is directly meaningful: S positive means buyers are transacting above sellers — they are paying up. That is strength, measured in the market's own units.

The flat-bar defect is fixed too

Trap 13 in the Field Manual: flat bars become matched atoms that inflate OVL, so a market that is not trading reads as “balanced”. Under flow separation a flat bar contributes the same price to both VWAPs, which leaves S unchanged rather than pushing it toward a false verdict — and because the statistic is in ticks, a dead market simply reports S ≈ 0 alongside an obviously tiny W₁, which reads as “nothing is happening” rather than as a regime call.

Algorithm 07

Volume-Clock Decay

replaces the last N bars that overlapped the frame, all weighted equally, with no age limit

A hard window has three defects at once: it weights a bar from an hour ago identically to the one printing now, it lurches when a bar falls out, and it will happily reach three thousand bars back. All three come from the same cause — a step function where a decay belongs.

w(bar)  =  2− Vsince ⁄ Vhalf Vsince is the volume traded since that bar; Vhalf is the half-life, expressed in multiples of average bar volume (default 4). Weights are maintained incrementally: one multiply per row per bar, no search, no window, no re-scan.

The clock is the crucial choice. Decaying in bars reproduces the current mistake in smoother form: a quiet overnight session ages out levels at the same rate as a violent hour. Decaying in volume means a level stays relevant until the market has actually done enough business to make it stale — which is the microstructure-correct notion of age and handles session boundaries for free.

Algorithm 08

Adaptive Frame

replaces ±5 ticks, a constant that means something different on every symbol

A frame measured in ticks is a frame measured in nothing. Measure it in volatility and it never needs tuning again.

viewTicks  =  round( κ · ATR(14) / tick )     κ default 1.0 κ = 1.0 makes the frame span roughly two average bar ranges, the sizing the Field Manual arrives at by hand in Chapter 12. The frame then self-adjusts across symbols, across sessions, and across volatility regimes, and the per-market preset table becomes a single number.

Pair it with the anchor modes the Field Manual already specifies — follow price, session open, or a manual level — and the frame becomes a deliberate choice rather than an accident of tick size. An anchored, volatility-scaled frame turns the window POC from a sliding local artefact into a level worth marking.

Algorithm 09

Level Efficiency — absorption versus initiative

adds a classification the instrument cannot currently express at all

High volume at a level means nothing on its own. High volume where price moved through is initiative; high volume where price stalled is absorption. They forecast opposite things, and the crossing decomposition already computes the number that separates them.

E(j)  =  | δ̄(j) | ⁄ N̄(j)  ∈  [0, 1] The same net and total crossings from Algorithm 02 — no new computation whatsoever. E near 1: the level was crossed once, decisively. E near 0: crossed many times, net nowhere.

This single classification is arguably worth more than every other item in this document, and it costs one division per row.

The redesign is not a trade of accuracy for compute. It is cheaper, because a null model replaces numerical machinery that existed only to compensate for not having one.

The one genuine cost increase is the occupancy recursion. At M = 32, R = 41 and a 23-bar profile that is roughly 30,000 multiply-adds per update — less than the current implementation spends on Simpson integration alone, and it replaces the quadrature, the bisection, the residual check, and the tolerance input all at once.

Engineering

What it still can’t do

Every one of these algorithms is an improvement over what it replaces. None of them is a substitute for data.

  • The bridge is still a model. Driftless, constant-volatility diffusion. Real intrabar dynamics have volatility clustering, jumps, and auction effects at the open and close. The occupancy model is a far better prior than two arbitrary bells — it is not the truth.
  • Volume is not proportional to crossings. The decomposition assumes it is. In reality volume clusters at round numbers, at the open and close, and at levels where resting liquidity happens to sit. The identity governing direction is exact; the mapping from crossings to size is a modelling choice.
  • None of this beats real footprint data. These algorithms make the modelled engines dramatically better and give the measured engine a proper null hypothesis. On a Premium plan, real rows still beat a good model, and Algorithm 03 exists precisely to compare the two rather than to replace them.
  • Prominence and HDR still need one threshold each. One arbitrary constant is replaced by another. The difference is that prominence has an interpretation — how far this peak stands above its saddle — whereas “300%” has none.
  • Overdispersion is estimated, not known. The φ in Algorithm 03 comes from the bar’s own rows, so it is noisy on bars with few active levels. Floor it at 1.0 and treat marginal z-scores with suspicion.
  • Volume-clock decay changes what a user is looking at. “The last five bars that traded here” is intuitive; “an exponentially weighted volume-clock average with a four-bar-volume half-life” is not. The algorithm is better and the explanation is worse — a real cost, not a rhetorical one.
  • Pine is a constrained host. The forward–backward recursion needs matrices sized (M+1)×R per bar. At large frames and long profile periods this will meet execution limits well before the mathematics runs out, and the adaptive guard in the Field Manual’s Chapter 27 becomes mandatory rather than optional.
